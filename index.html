01The problem

AML analysts review 200+ flagged alerts per day using software built when dial-up was the standard. They're burning out — and criminals are getting through.

Oracle FCCM, SAS AML, Actimize — the dominant tools in this space have interfaces that treat analysts like database operators, not investigators. Context is scattered across 4 different screens. Pattern recognition is left entirely to human memory. Every case decision requires switching between tabs, copying case IDs by hand, and writing notes in a separate Word document. The cognitive load is unsustainable — and the cost is real: analysts miss suspicious patterns, cases breach SLA deadlines, and compliance managers have no visibility into the backlog until it's too late.

200+
Alerts reviewed per analyst per day in large Indian banks. At this volume, pattern recognition from memory alone is impossible.
4

023 user types — 3 mental models

One system. Three completely different jobs to be done.

Senior designers design for user types, not personas. Each role has a distinct workflow, a different relationship with data, and different definition of what "done" means.

L1 Analyst
Alert Triage Specialist
Processes 200+ alerts daily. Needs to quickly classify each as suspicious or false positive. Values: speed, context-at-a-glance, one-click decisions. Cognitive load is their enemy.
L2 Investigator
Deep Investigation Lead
Handles escalated cases requiring deep analysis. Needs entity relationship mapping, full transaction history, document management. Values: comprehensiveness, traceability, evidence quality.
Compliance Manager
Team & Regulatory Oversight
Monitors team SLAs, case backlogs, SAR filing rates. Needs team performance metrics, regulatory deadline tracking, audit readiness. Values: control, predictability, regulatory confidence.

05Design process

From domain knowledge to production design.

This project is built on real professional experience — I designed AML compliance tooling in a previous engagement. Nexus is what I would build with full creative freedom: no legacy constraints, no database-first thinking, pure UX logic.

01Domain research

Starting from real AML knowledge

My foundation is practical: I have designed compliance dashboards in a professional AML engagement (under NDA). Before sketching Nexus, I documented what I knew — the alert lifecycle, the escalation workflow, SAR filing requirements under PMLA, the role of FIU-IND, and the specific cognitive challenges of shift-based alert review. Nexus is informed by domain expertise, not just design principles.

Nexus case study interview answer: "I have professional experience designing AML tooling, which gave me deep domain knowledge about alert workflows and compliance requirements. This project represents my vision of what the category could become."
02User research

4 analyst interviews — surfacing the real workflow

Four informal conversations with banking compliance professionals at different experience levels. The recurring theme: every analyst had developed personal workarounds — Excel notes, paper sketches of entity networks, WhatsApp messages to managers. These workarounds are not laziness. They are evidence that the existing tools fail to support the actual workflow. Every workaround became a Nexus feature.

03Figma AI Make

AI-generated layout foundations — then completely rebuilt

Used Figma AI Make to generate initial layout structures for the 3-column case detail screen and the alert queue table. The AI correctly identified the general structure (list on left, detail in centre, actions on right) but consistently failed on the compliance-specific details: it showed generic data tables rather than risk-colour-coded rows, generic text inputs rather than decision radio buttons with audit implications, and no concept of the SLA deadline as a first-class UI element.

What AI built: skeleton. What I built: the AML-specific logic — risk vocabulary, keyboard shortcuts, audit trail integration, SLA urgency communication. Every compliance-specific decision in Nexus is mine.
04Design system

A risk colour vocabulary — not just a palette

The Nexus colour system is semantic, not aesthetic. Critical: #EF4444 red — used only for imminent threats and overdue SLAs. High: #F59E0B amber — elevated risk, requires attention today. Medium: #EAB308 yellow — monitor, not urgent. Low: #10B981 green — baseline, no action needed. Blue #3B82F6: primary UI, information, system actions. This vocabulary is documented as a rule — no element uses these colours for decorative purposes. When everything urgent is red, red means something.

05Figma

5 screens — each solving a distinct workflow problem

Alert Queue (L1 triage speed), Case Detail (L2 investigation context), Entity Graph (relationship pattern recognition), Compliance Manager Dashboard (leadership visibility), Audit Trail (regulatory traceability). Each screen was designed for its user type — different information density, different primary actions, different definition of "task complete."

Progressive disclosure — show 3 data points, hide the rest until needed
Junior designers show everything. Senior designers hide what isn't needed right now. The alert queue shows only the data needed to decide whether to open a case — severity, entity, type, risk score, amount, status. Everything else lives one click away in the case detail. This reduces cognitive load from 12 data points to 6, enabling faster triage.
UX principle: Progressive disclosure

Filtered network graph — 6 highest-risk nodes, not 50
A fully expanded entity network with 50 nodes is visually impressive and completely useless. An analyst doesn't need to see every connection — they need to see the highest-risk connections, and understand why they're high risk. Nexus filters to the 8 most significant connected entities by default, with a "Show all" expand option. The right panel immediately shows the selected entity's risk score, watchlist status, and flagged transaction count. The graph is navigation, not decoration.
UX principle: Contextual filtering

Audit trail as a first-class feature — not a compliance afterthought
Most AML tools log user actions in a database table that only the tech team can access. Nexus surfaces the audit trail as a navigable, searchable, anomaly-detecting screen that compliance managers can access directly. The anomaly detection banner — "47 cases reviewed in 90 minutes" — surfaces potential data integrity issues before they become regulatory problems. The "Read-only" badge in the top bar is a deliberate design decision: it signals immutability as a trust property, not a limitation.
UX principle: Transparency as trust
075 decisions that prove senior thinking

These are the decisions most designers never make — because most designers have never designed for financial crime.

01
Progressive disclosure on the alert queue
Show only 6 data points per alert in the list view. Everything else appears when the case is opened. Junior designers show all 12 available data fields. Senior designers ask: what does the analyst need to decide whether to open this case? Only that. Nothing else.
Cognitive load reduction
02
Risk score with reasoning, not just a number
"Risk score: 94 — Unusual cash deposits + cross-border transfers + PEP relationship" enables decision-making. "Risk score: 94" enables guessing. An analyst cannot file a legally defensible Suspicious Activity Report based on a number with no explanation. Nexus surfaces the contributing factors inline at every point where a decision is made.
Explanatory design
03
Keyboard-first interaction design — documented as a feature
AML analysts review hundreds of cases per shift. Mouse-only interfaces are physically exhausting and cognitively inefficient at that volume. Nexus is designed keyboard-first: Tab to next alert, E to escalate, D to dismiss, N to add note, G to open entity graph. This is accessibility design and power-user design simultaneously — documented explicitly in the design system as a keyboard shortcut vocabulary.
Accessibility · Power user
04
Entity graph filtered to 8 nodes by default
A network graph with 50 nodes showing every connection an entity has ever had is visually impressive and analytically useless. Nexus defaults to showing the 8 highest-risk connections, filtered by risk score. Users can expand to see all connections with one click. This is the difference between information overload and actionable intelligence — a distinction that takes years of working in complex data environments to internalise.
Contextual filtering · Data visualisation
05
Audit trail with AI anomaly detection — surfaced to managers
Every compliance system logs actions. Nexus is the first to make the audit log navigable and intelligent. The anomaly detection layer — "47 cases reviewed in 90 minutes" — surfaces patterns that indicate either a data integrity problem or analyst misconduct before they become regulatory issues. The read-only badge signals immutability as a trust property. The compliance manager can view it directly, not through the IT team.
Proactive compliance design

09If Nexus shipped

The metrics I would track from day one.

< 5 min
Avg triage time per alert
Current industry average: 12-15 minutes per alert. Nexus's unified context view targets 5 minutes through progressive disclosure and keyboard workflows.
< 2%
SLA breach rate
Real-time manager visibility replaces reactive WhatsApp notification. Target: less than 2% of cases breach their deadline — down from 8-12% in typical installations.
> 95%
SAR defensibility rate
Risk score with explanation + audit trail = every SAR submission is fully documented. Target: 95%+ of SARs withstand FIU-IND scrutiny without rework.
< 30s
Context gathering time
Currently: analysts switch between 4 screens and take 12-15 minutes to gather case context. Nexus's 3-column case detail eliminates switching entirely. Target: under 30 seconds.
> 4.5
Analyst satisfaction NPS
AML tooling has some of the lowest user satisfaction scores in enterprise software. A tool that respects how analysts actually work should score significantly above industry average (typically 2-3).
0
Audit trail integrity issues
AI anomaly detection on the audit trail surfaces data integrity issues before they reach regulators. Target: zero undetected anomalies per quarter. This is a compliance outcome, not a UX metric — and that's the point.
